Why it stops at the octave level

The integral runs along a straight line, and a straight line goes through walls. Two points on opposite sides of a wall differ in the row by the time to cross the wall, not the time to go around it. Speed is at its floor everywhere near a wall, which is over a third of the maze, so the row climbs as fast along a wall as across one. Measured on the rows before any training, the difference across a wall divided by the difference across an open passage is {{scr_int22}} for the 30-term integral and {{scr_exact22}} for the exact one. Speed rows give {{scr_true22}} and distance rows {{scr_geo22}}.

The trained fields agree. With integral rows the jump is {{p2jump_int22}}, and pinning the rows leaves it at {{p2fjump_int22}}, so the octave rows still carry all the separation. Distance rows are the only ones that carry it themselves ({{jump_geo22}} intact, {{fjump_geo22}} frozen). The integral rows are smooth ramps across the maze, which makes them position-like features, and position is what the octaves already supply.
