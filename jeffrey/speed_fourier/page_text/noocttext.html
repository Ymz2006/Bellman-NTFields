• The walls in the integral do help. The control replaces 1 / speed with 1, so each row is just the length along its line and holds no walls. It reaches {{p3_unit13}} % and {{p3_unit22}} %, so the real integral is {{p3d_exactunit13}} points on 13×13 and {{p3d_exactunit22}} on 22×22. The 30-term integral read at (−x, −y) reaches {{p3_intflip13}} % and {{p3_intflip22}} %, so the true placement is {{p3d_intintflip13}} and {{p3d_intintflip22}} points.
  • It still does not reach the octaves. The exact integral is {{p3d_exactoct13}} points from the octaves on 13×13 and {{p3d_exactoct22}} on 22×22. The 30-term integral reaches {{p3_int13}} % and {{p3_int22}} %.
  • The per-term matrix fails. One channel per sin/cos term, 8 directions of 30 channels, reaches {{p3_iterms13}} % and {{p3_iterms22}} %. Flipping the walls changes nothing ({{p3_itermsflip13}} % and {{p3_itermsflip22}} %), so the failure comes from the shape of the channels, not from the maze.
  • Integral plus speed rows fails. 120 integral rows with 120 speed rows reach {{p3_both13}} % and {{p3_both22}} %, close to speed rows alone ({{p3_speed13}} % and {{p3_speed22}} %). The integral rows alone do far better, so the speed rows are what break it.

Why it stays at the level of random rows

Each integral row is a ramp across the maze with steps at the walls. Across 240 directions the ramps are nearly the same two coordinates seen from different angles. The effective rank of the embedding is {{p3rank_exact13}} on 13×13, against {{p3rank_oct13}} for the octaves and {{p3rank_ntrl13}} for ntrl-demo's random rows. The network gets a smooth position code with wall detail on it, and no ladder of scales to build sharp walls from.

The trained fields show the two halves of that. With no walls in the rows, the 22×22 field is smooth and has the right scale (T / T* {{p3t_unit22}}, {{p3bumps_unit22}} bumps per path), but {{p3leak_unit22}} of wall segments have no rise at all. With the real integral the walls appear (only {{p3leak_exact22}} of segments without a rise), but the field is compressed (T / T* {{p3t_exact22}}), the difference across a wall is {{p3jump_exact22}} of the true one against {{p3jump_oct22}} for the octaves, and T has {{p3bumps_exact22}} local minima per path. On 13×13 the exact-integral field is close to correct in scale and separation ({{p3t_exact13}} and {{p3jump_exact13}}), and it plans like ntrl-demo.

The three failing embeddings share one signature. Local speed is fitted well (correlation {{p3fit_iterms13}} for the per-term matrix), but T is a tenth of the true time, the difference across walls is zero, and the descent direction is unrelated to the true path (cosine {{p3cos_iterms13}}). Rank does not explain it: the per-term matrix has rank {{p3rank_iterms13}}, the highest of any embedding here. I have not found the cause. Each of the three contains channels that follow the local speed pattern, either speed rows or single terms of the fit. Each also contains ramps, and the ramps do not rescue it.

These are one training seed each, and seed noise on these mazes is several points. The 40-point gap to the octaves and the collapse of the matrix are far outside that. The gains from the walls on 22×22 are not.
