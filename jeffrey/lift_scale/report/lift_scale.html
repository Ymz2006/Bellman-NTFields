The lift holds at every size; lift + push works where walls are thick, and finer position features give thin walls the room they lack

The lift and the push were rebuilt on this workstation and run for a rectangle robot (x, y, heading) on plus mazes of 22 to 40 cells and Gibson floor plans of 32 to 96 cells. The lift’s own claim holds everywhere: S × |∇T| is 0.93–1.02 in the innermost band on every scene (ntrl-demo 0.05–0.11). The lift alone plans below ntrl-demo on 7 of 8 scenes: obstacles are seen through, and in this space the far field also comes out short, because the eikonal equation can be satisfied with slope along the heading coordinate. With the push, plus 22 / plus 30 / plus 40 / apartment 32 / Allensville 43 / apartment 64 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96: 68.2 / 64.2 / 63.2 / 75.4 / 99.6 / 73.0 / 76.5 / 85.5 % of queries solved against 65.0 / 56.8 / 59.2 / 76.8 / 90.3 / 66.3 / 69.1 / 71.2 % for ntrl-demo and 89 / 81 / 79 / 38 / 88 / 37 / 87 / 94 % of travel times within 10 % against 46 / 61 / 52 / 47 / 78 / 20 / 74 / 77 %, with almost no plan ending in a wall. Where it stops short, the detours behind thin walls are seen through, and the tests here say why: the push’s detours are slope violations (with the slope cap enforced there are none), and even with the wall interiors trained the learned time across a wall is 1.3 cells where crossing costs 56. A wall is a symmetric bump in the lifted input; the true field needs a step across the wall’s own thickness in the position coordinates. In the houses that step fits where the obstacle is thick: in the 96-cell house lift + push sees through 100 % of the detour pairs whose thickest obstacle is 1–1.5 cells and 4 % of those at 4–8 cells. Finer position features (octaves scaled with the scene) give thinner obstacles the same room. With them lift + push solves 99.9 / 97.9 / 98.7 % of the three houses with 83 / 98 / 98 % of travel times within 10 % (ntrl-demo 90.3 / 69.1 / 71.2 %), and 76.2 / 68.8 % of the 30- and 40-cell mazes with 95 / 93 % within 10 % (ntrl-demo 56.8 / 59.2 %). What was run

The study folder of the lift page is not on this workstation, so the lift and the push were rebuilt here from the page’s description, on the scenes, planner and scorer every local maze study uses. Two things differ from that page and are part of the test.

  • A configuration space, not the plane. The robot is a 0.5 × 0.2-cell rectangle with heading, q = (x, y, φ); the clearance is the rectangle’s exact signed distance and ntrl-demo’s speed is computed from it (1 beyond 0.175 cells, floor 0.012). The lift is the same one-dimensional function of that clearance, h = G(d(q)), as one more input column. In this space the clearance gradient is not a unit vector (median 1.07–1.16 times one, 90th percentile 1.35–2.12), the case the lift page lists as not tested.
  • Larger scenes. Plus mazes of 22, 30 and 40 cells a side, the P-NTFields apartment at 32 and 64 cells, and three Gibson houses cut into floor plans at 0.25 m per cell (43, 83 and 96 cells a side). 500,000 of ntrl-demo’s own training pairs per scene, 5,000 epochs, ntrl-demo unchanged on disk.

Obstacles for the robot’s centre at heading 0 (axes in cells). The houses are slices of the scanned meshes at five heights between 0.4 and 1.6 m above the floor, furniture included; everything outside the largest sealed interior is an obstacle.

Arms, each trained and planned the same way (MPPI, planning seeds 0 / 1 / 2, success = arrives with the exact clearance never negative):

  • ntrl-demo, the control. The no-lift arm of this code reproduces the registered ntrl-demo run of the same seed to every printed digit of the loss.
  • lift: the extra input h, nothing else.
  • lift + push: the selection term of the lift page with its schedule (λ faded in from epoch 200, 1e-2 from 600 to 2,000, relaxed to 3e-3 by 3,000; one-sided slope term μ = 0.1; pairs pushed while T is below 3 box lengths). A pair is pushed when its straight segment crosses an obstacle; that is 62–85 % of the training pairs here.
  • push without the lift, the second control.
  • lift + push + octaves (from Part 5 on): the same with 48 of ntrl-demo’s 128 Fourier rows replaced by whitened rows at ×2, ×4 and ×8 the base frequency, times the box side over 22 cells.

Field measures against the grid solver’s travel time T*: the share of random (free configuration, goal) pairs within 10 %; the same on pairs with a free line of sight; and the detour index ρ = (T − D) / (T* − D) on pairs whose true path is at least 30 % longer than the straight distance D (0 = the field is the straight distance, 1 = the truth). A detour pair counts as seen through when ρ is below 0.5.

What the lift is, on a floor plan

For every configuration the network gets one more input, h = G(d) / E: d is the robot’s clearance (the minimum of the floor plan’s signed-distance table over a 9 × 5 lattice of points on the rectangle), and G(d) is the travel time from that clearance straight out of the slow band, a one-dimensional table computed from the speed profile. Nothing else changes: the same Fourier features with one more column, the same loss, differentiated with respect to the configuration through ∂h/∂q = G′(d) ∇d / E.

Part 1 · The lift itself holds: the wall layer is exact at every size

The lift’s own claim carries over unchanged. S × |∇T| on held-out band points is 0.93–1.02 in the innermost band on every scene with the lift alone, against 0.05–0.11 for ntrl-demo, whose fit gets worse as the scene grows (the band is the same 0.16 cells, a smaller share of the network’s input). It is there early: 1.02 / 0.97 / 1.01 / 1.19 / 1.23 / 1.10 / 1.44 / 1.31 at epoch 100 and 0.97 / 0.96 / 0.96 / 1.01 / 0.97 / 0.90 / 1.04 / 1.00 at epoch 1,000 on plus 22 / plus 30 / plus 40 / apartment 32 / Allensville 43 / apartment 64 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96 (on the floor plans it settles from above). The clearance gradient not being a unit vector costs nothing measurable; the lift equalizes the normal speed to within that factor, which is at most about 1.4 here, where the unlifted profile spans 83.

With the push the fit drops to 0.78–0.86: the one-sided term pulls slopes under 1 across the band while the push is on. That is a property of the push as specified, not of the lift (Part 4).

Part 2 · The lift alone: obstacles are seen through, and in this space the far field also comes out short

As on the lift page, an exact wall layer does not make a field that plans. With the lift alone 98 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 100 % of the detour pairs are seen through (plus 22 / plus 30 / plus 40 / apartment 32 / Allensville 43 / apartment 64 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96) and no plan ends inside a wall: every failure is a stall (45 / 43 / 44 / 37 / 33 / 38 / 46 / 40 % of the plans), on the mazes at a point pressed against a wall with the goal straight behind it, on the floor plans mostly in open space (Part 7). ntrl-demo fails the other way on the mazes, by arriving through walls (15 / 28 / 40 % of plans on the plus mazes).

What is new in the configuration space is that the lifted field is also too short along free lines of sight, which the 2-D fields of the lift page were not. Learned / true time on line-of-sight pairs is 0.88 / 0.73 / 0.73 / 0.67 / 0.73 / 0.73 / 0.54 / 0.55 with the lift alone (plus 22 / plus 30 / plus 40 / apartment 32 / Allensville 43 / apartment 64 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96; ntrl-demo 1.10 / 1.08 / 1.09 / 0.99 / 1.02 / 0.94 / 0.98 / 0.97), and only 44 / 22 / 20 / 17 / 13 / 18 / 5 / 7 % of those pairs are within 10 %. The slope is not missing: |∇T| is 0.95 on the 22-cell maze even beyond 16 cells. It points the wrong way. The heading coordinate φ spans 0.39 cells in all, so a field can satisfy the eikonal equation with part of its slope along φ and gain almost no travel time from it.

On the 22-cell maze the lifted field’s radial slope falls from 1.00 within 2 cells of the goal to 0.63 beyond 16, while its heading slope rises from 0.20 to 0.55 (ntrl-demo: radial 0.93, heading 0.15). In the 32-cell apartment the radial slope is down to 0.57. This is one more member of the family the lift page describes: a field with the right slope everywhere that is smaller than the true one. ntrl-demo’s smeared wall layer leaves it no such freedom; once the lift makes the local fit exact, nothing in the loss holds the far field up.

Control. A lifted network whose Fourier column for φ is zero cannot put slope there (it still sees heading through h, which depends on it). Trained like the lift arm, its line-of-sight times are right without any push: learned / true 1.00, 97 % within 10 % (lift 44 %), radial slope 0.94 beyond 16 cells. It is a control, not a recipe: a field that cannot see heading plans 34 % and it is as see-through as the lift (98 % of detour pairs).

The push removes the short far field at once: line-of-sight pairs within 10 % go to 99 / 100 / 100 / 99 / 98 / 99 / 100 / 99 % (plus 22 / plus 30 / plus 40 / apartment 32 / Allensville 43 / apartment 64 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96; lift alone 44 / 22 / 20 / 17 / 13 / 18 / 5 / 7 %, ntrl-demo 51 / 66 / 60 / 87 / 88 / 70 / 84 / 87 %), with the heading slope back to 0.04 beyond 16 cells on the 22-cell maze. Over all random pairs 89 / 81 / 79 / 38 / 88 / 37 / 87 / 94 % of travel times are within 10 % (ntrl-demo 46 / 61 / 52 / 47 / 78 / 20 / 74 / 77 %). The part the push was added for holds on some scenes and not on others: 53 / 91 / 100 / 100 / 0 / 100 / 54 / 21 % of the detour pairs are still seen through at the end of training. The houses, whose walls are thick, keep most of their detours (Part 7); the plus mazes and the apartment, whose walls are thin, do not. The rest of this part and Parts 4 to 6 are about the thin-walled scenes.

The training course shows where the detours go. On the 22-cell maze the detour index reaches 1.64 at epoch 2,000, the end of the strong push, and the field then plans 87 % of the queries (planning seed 0), including 80 % of the longest bin that no final field solves. Its line-of-sight times are 12 % long at that point. When the push is relaxed the index falls to 0.45 within 1,000 epochs and ends at 0.46; planning is back to 68.2 %. This is the lift page’s plus-maze observation (index 1.66 at epoch 2,000, 0.50 at 3,000), reproduced in a different space with different code. The earlier run of the same recipe, whose one-sided term was not faded in, reached only 1.08 at epoch 2,000 and planned 69 % there: the state at the end of the strong push is not a reproducible one.

A direct look at one wall: for mirror configurations on its two sides, 0.8 cells apart, the true travel times to a goal differ by the whole detour when the wall separates them. ntrl-demo holds such a step almost exactly on the 22-cell maze (a true step of 13.5 cells is learned as 13.5); the lift alone holds 0.8 cells; lift + push holds 18.3 at epoch 2,000 and 3.0 at the end.

Part 4 · Why the detours behind thin walls do not hold: a wall is a bump in the lifted input, and the push cannot make it a barrier

Four tests on the 22-cell plus maze (one seed each, same data and schedule length) separate what the push does from what the field can represent.

1. A stronger push buys slope violations, not a stable field

Kept at its strong value for the whole training (λ = 1e-2), the push does not hold the detours either. The detour index swings between checkpoints, 1.02 → 0.67 → 0.54 → 1.17 → 1.01 → 0.33 from epoch 1,000 to 5,000, the line-of-sight times stay 8–12 % long, ntrl-demo’s epoch-repeat rule rolls back 404 times, and the final field plans 68.2 % with 51 % of times within 10 %. The slope cap of the recipe is a quadratic penalty: its gradient vanishes at the constraint, so any push is paid for with a violation proportional to its strength, everywhere, and the detours that appear are made of that violation.

2. With the slope cap enforced, the push builds no detours at all

Adding a linear (exact) one-sided penalty, whose gradient does not vanish at the constraint, to the same strong push: the line-of-sight times stay at 0.96 of the truth, and the detour index is 0.00 at the end, with 100 % of the detour pairs seen through (60.3 % solved). A stiff quadratic cap (μ = 10) gives the same: index -0.02. So the largest field that respects the slope cap at the trained points is the see-through field. The detours of the soft-cap runs existed only as violations. (The linear cap has a cost of its own: it drags the band slopes to 0.76 of the eikonal value.)

3. The reason: nothing separates the two sides of a wall

ntrl-demo samples no point deeper than 0.0175 cells inside an obstacle, so across a wall the eikonal loss says nothing, and a network keeps its output continuous there. That much the lift page already names. The test here goes one step further: train the inside as well. With one end of 20 % of the training pairs inside obstacles (floor speed there, the registered data variant of the embedding study) and the lift continued through the walls at the floor slope, the interior becomes as learnable as the band: S × |∇T| is 1.00–1.03 at every depth inside the walls (ntrl-demo: 0.03–0.16). The equation now holds everywhere, and the field is still see-through:

between two configurations that face each other across a wall, 0.8 cells apart, the learned travel time is 1.3 cells (median), where going straight through costs 56 cells at the floor speed and going around costs the detour. The field climbs the wall at the right slope from one side and descends it at the right slope on the other. In the lifted input that is the natural thing to do: h depends on the clearance alone, so the two faces of a wall have the same height and the wall is a symmetric bump, T ≈ T0(x) + h with T0 smooth across it. That function satisfies the eikonal equation of even the closed problem almost everywhere. The true field has the same shape and differs only in T0: a step of the size of the detour across the wall’s own thickness, at equal height. That step has to be built in the position coordinates, inside 0.3–0.6 cells, and it is the one thing the lift gives no input length for.

The short far field of Part 2 is gone without any push (line-of-sight learned / true 1.03, 92 % within 10 %), and the detour index creeps up by itself, to 0.45 after 5,000 epochs and 0.54 after 12,500, where it wanders between 0.3 and 0.6. Its detours saturate where the planar step runs out: a true detour of 8.0 cells is learned as 4.1, one of 13.3 as 4.2.

The same arm on the 64-cell apartment (one seed) changes little: 63.6 % solved, 7 % of times within 10 %, 24 % along lines of sight (the lift with ntrl-demo’s pairs: 62.1 %, 5 %, 18 %). There the walls are a small part of a large open floor, and training their interiors does not hold the far field up; the push does.

A coordinate that tells the two sides apart was tried in the same setting: the vector lift, h times the unit clearance gradient (three extra inputs), which is the same lift along a normal and gives the two faces of a wall opposite vectors. With inside pairs it plans 58.6 % without a push and 59.4 % with the page’s push, detour index 0.16 and 0.16. The vectors separate the two sides inside the wall and within a few hundredths of a cell of its faces; free configurations on the two sides, where the step has to show, are still 0.7 cells apart in the input. What is missing is room in the position coordinates themselves.

4. Why plain ntrl-demo has detours on the small maze and loses them on the large ones

ntrl-demo cannot draw the thin wall layer, so its slope is high through the whole band on both sides of a wall, and that excess is what carries the step: on the 22-cell maze it holds a true step of 13.5 cells across a wall as 13.5, with travel times 10 % long everywhere. The lift removes exactly that excess. And the excess does not scale: on the 40-cell maze the same network holds 0.8 cells of a 14-cell step (1.1 of 7.8), and arrives through walls on 40 % of its plans.

Part 5 · Testing that explanation: finer position features

If the missing piece is planar resolution across a wall, more of it should make the push’s detours real. The embedding study’s octaves do that without a map: 48 of ntrl-demo’s 128 Fourier rows are replaced by whitened rows at ×2, ×4 and ×8 the base frequency (network size unchanged; the lift column is untouched). Part 7 · Gibson floor plans

The two kinds of floor plan behave differently. The apartment is a scan outline: walls a quarter of a cell thick at 64 cells, with free space on both sides and around the outside. The houses have walls with their real thickness, a closed outside and furniture.

The apartment: every field is see-through

ntrl-demo itself sees through 65 / 100 % of the detour pairs here (apartment 32 / apartment 64) and fails by stalling (21 / 32 % of plans, 3 / 1 % through walls); its far field is a little short as well (0.99 / 0.94 of the truth along lines of sight).

The lift alone makes it worse, as on the mazes: 62.9 / 62.1 % solved against 76.8 / 66.3 %, with 17 / 18 % of line-of-sight times within 10 %; its plans stall in the open middle of rooms where the short field has a minimum (at 64 cells 92 % of its stalls are more than half a cell from any wall). Lift + push plans 75.4 / 73.0 %: the far field is right (99 / 99 % of line-of-sight times within 10 %), no plan ends in a wall, and the stalls that remain are pressed against a wall with the goal behind it. But every detour pair is still seen through, and unlike on the mazes nothing tried here changes that: octaves scaled with the scene 72.8 % solved, one octave higher 73.7 %, the strong push kept to the end 73.6 % (detour index 0.12), ntrl-demo with octaves 68.5 %. This scene is the open case of the study.

The houses: lift + push works, and with octaves it solves them

The three houses (Allensville 43 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96 cells) are kinder to ntrl-demo than the apartment: their walls have real thickness and the outside is closed, and ntrl-demo’s travel times are already 78 / 74 / 77 % within 10 % with a detour index of 1.19 / 0.67 / 0.80. With octaves scaled to the scene, lift + push on Allensville 43 / Lakeville 83 / Klickitat 96: 99.9 / 97.9 / 98.7 % solved, 83 / 98 / 98 % of travel times within 10 %, 0.1 / 1.1 / 0.9 % of detour pairs seen through, 0.1 / 2.1 / 1.3 % of plans stopped short and none through a wall. The octaves alone do not do this (ntrl-demo with the same octaves: – / 65.6 / 71.4 % solved, – / 44 / 53 % within 10 %); the lift, the push and the octaves each remove a different failure: wall hits, the short and see-through far field, and the thin obstacles.

The houses give the explanation of Part 4 a natural test, because their obstacles come in every thickness: furniture and partitions under a cell, walls with their unreachable interiors of several cells, whole blocks of rooms. Grouping the detour pairs by the thickest single obstacle their straight line crosses, lift + push in Klickitat sees through 100 % of the pairs whose thickest obstacle is 1–1.5 cells, 62 % at 1.5–2.5, 14 % at 2.5–4 and 4 % at 4–8 cells (detour index 1.00 there). The plus mazes and the apartment have nothing but thin walls, and there everything is seen through. So the recipe of the lift page works as specified where an obstacle is thick enough for the step across it to fit in the position coordinates, and fails where it is not; which scenes work is decided by their wall thickness in cells, not by their size. With the octaves the turn moves to thinner obstacles: Klickitat 0 % seen through at 1–1.5 cells and 1 % at 1.5–2.5, Lakeville 3 % and 2 %.

Tried and not adopted

  • Vector lift (h times the unit clearance gradient, three extra inputs, so that the two faces of a wall get opposite vectors). With ntrl-demo’s pairs and the push: 62.5 % solved, detour index 0.22 (22-cell maze). With inside pairs and the lift continued through the walls: 59.4 % with the push, 58.6 % without. The vectors differ inside the wall and close to its faces only; free configurations on the two sides are still 0.7 cells apart in the input.
  • One octave higher (×4, ×8, ×16 times the scene’s scale) under lift + push: 40-cell maze 66.4 % solved against 68.8 % with the standard octaves; 64-cell apartment 73.7 % against 72.8 %.
  • A weak constant push with the exact cap (λ = 3e-3): 59.7 % solved, detour index -0.03. Three times the strong push with a stiff cap and inside pairs: 60.4 %, index 0.01.
  • The one-sided term at full weight from the first push epoch (the first two runs): ntrl-demo’s epoch-repeat rule rolled back 1,378 times, almost all in the first 100 push epochs; the result is the same as with the faded term (66.4 % solved). The term is faded in with the push in every other run.

Where this leaves the recipe

  • The lift is safe to keep at any size and in a configuration space: one extra input from the clearance the speed labels already use, an exact wall layer, no plans through walls. Alone it is not a planner.
  • The far field needs something more than the eikonal loss once the wall layer is exact, and more so with a short configuration coordinate. The push supplies it within a few hundred epochs; training the wall interiors supplies it too, with no push.
  • The detours need room in the position coordinates across an obstacle. Thick walls have it, and there lift + push works as the lift page specifies it. For thinner obstacles the lift page used a wall-magnifying planar map; here octaves of the position features, scaled with the scene, do that job with no map. On the houses they complete the recipe (98–100 % solved with the page’s schedule, travel times right). On the thin-walled mazes they make the push’s detours stable, and how much of them survives is set by how strong the push is kept, which is the accuracy-for-planning trade the lift page describes: the schedule that relaxes to 3e-3 gives the accurate end, a push that stays at 1e-2 the planning end. The apartment is the exception to all of this.
  • Not needed here: a denser collision check for the push (the 48-point check misses 0.2–1.6 % of the blocked pairs), a larger bound (the largest true travel time between free configurations is 0.9–1.9 box lengths), the vector lift.

Limits

  • Most variants are one training seed, and one training moves by 3–6 points. The findings above that rest on single runs are the ones with large effects (detour index 0.0 against 1.0 and more, 91 % against 68 %); differences of a few points between final fields are not claimed.
  • Under a strong push the field keeps changing: with octaves and a constant push of 6e-3 the same run plans 72 %, 95 %, 72 % and 72 % at epochs 1,000, 2,000, 3,000 and 5,000. A final number of such a run is one sample of that motion.
  • The lift page’s scenes (point robots in the plane) and its wall-magnifying map are not on this machine, so the map could not be put next to the octaves, and the difference between the plane and the configuration space is inferred from the heading measurement and its control, not from a matched pair of scenes.
  • On the plus mazes the longest queries, across two or more quadrants, are solved only by strong-push fields. The houses are slices of scans at fixed heights with their furniture; the apartment’s outside is free space, the houses’ is not.
  • The 64-cell apartment is not explained. Its walls are a quarter of a cell thick against a ground-truth grid of an eighth of a cell: 95 of 448 candidate queries were dropped at preparation because their true path crossed a wall, so the detour index there is measured against a truth that is itself partly through walls. The queries that are planned are certified collision-free.
