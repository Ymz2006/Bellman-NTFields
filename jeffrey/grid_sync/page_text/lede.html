ntrl-demo describes each pose to its network with random Fourier features, and on mazes larger than its 7×7 demo those features are too coarse to resolve a cell. Tuning them to the maze grid fixes most of that, but only with more than one scale. Grid-synced octaves (half periods of 1, 2, 4, … cells, each wave in step with the grid lines) solve {{succ:octave:13}} of the 13×13 runs and {{succ:octave:22}} of the 22×22 runs, against {{succ:ntrl-demo:13}} and {{succ:ntrl-demo:22}} for ntrl-demo and {{succ:best B × s:13}} and {{succ:best B × s:22}} for the best hand-tuned frequency scale. The lock to the grid is a small part of that gain: over the same octaves with their magnitudes shuffled out of step, it sharpens the walls and adds {{lock:octaveperm:13}} points on 13×13 and {{lock:octaveperm:22}} on 22×22 (mean of paired training seeds). The single-frequency version, every wave at one cell per half period, draws walls sharply but is erratic: it helps on 10×10 and 13×13 and collapses on 7×7 and 22×22.
