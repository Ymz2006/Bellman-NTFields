• Does it make ntrl-demo more accurate? Yes, once the features cover more than one scale. Grid-synced octaves give the most accurate field and the best planning on every maze from 10×10 up, with no frequency to tune: the grid sets them.
  • Does the resonance itself matter? A little, and more on the larger maze. Locking each wave to the grid sharpens the walls in every pair measured; over the same waves out of step it adds {{lock:octaveperm:13}} points on 13×13 and {{lock:octaveperm:22}} on 22×22, and nothing measurable on 10×10 where every octave field is near 100 %. Most of the gain over ntrl-demo comes from putting waves at the cell scale at all.
  • The single-frequency version is erratic. One cell per half period for every wave sharpens walls and helps on 10×10 and 13×13 ({{succ:ring:10}}, {{succ:ring:13}}), but nothing in it builds maze-scale distances directly: on 7×7 and 22×22 its long-range field collapses ({{succ:ring:7}}, {{succ:ring:22}}).
  • Which cos? For every grid line to see the same phase, the frequency has to grow as 1/cosθ′ (crests one cell apart along the axis). The other reading, half period = cell/cosθ′, keeps a traveller along the wave in step with the grid crossings but not the grid itself; it was run as literal (results table).
