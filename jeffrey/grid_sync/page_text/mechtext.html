Why the single frequency fails. With every wave at the grid frequency the network has to build maze-scale distances out of products of cell-scale waves. On 10×10 and 13×13 it manages; on 7×7 its travel time stops growing after about two cells (T/T* 0.27, although its local speed fit is the best of any field, 0.97), and the 22×22 field is ripple: {{bumps:ring:22}} local minima of T along each long true path (ntrl-demo: {{bumps:ntrl-demo:22}}), most of them at the cell centres, where 21 % of the field's cell-scale error is explained by the position within the cell. The walls fade (learned speed 0.35 cells from a wall {{wallS:ring:22}}, truth 0.22).

What the lock does. The synced octaves and the shuffled control have the same magnitudes and directions; only the lock differs. The synced fields have the sharper wall barrier in every pair (22×22 near-wall slope {{slope:octave:22}} against {{slope:octaveperm:22}}, seed 87358; 0.62 vs 0.55 and 0.63 vs 0.57 for seeds 1 and 2) and hit fewer walls, while their local-minimum counts are the same (116 vs 106 over three seeds on 22×22). A wave in step with the grid has the same phase at every grid line, so a sharp edge built at one line is the same edge at all of them; out of step, the edge pattern drifts across the maze. Some of the avoided wall hits become stalls instead of successes, the same trade ntrl-demo's wall term made in the maze-scale study. Two other explanations for the stalls were tested and ruled out: the octave fields' cell-scale error is not locked to the grid (under 1 % of it is explained by the position within the cell), and their minima are not concentrated at wall corners any more than the controls'.
