How it is computed

  • Lot: the unit square with 0.02 outer walls; bays open toward −x at x = 0.10 and run to the back wall at 0.48. The car is 0.30 long (along x) and parks centred at x = 0.29, θ = 0.
  • Clearance d: the exact distance between the car rectangle and the nearest wall box (negative = how far it overlaps).
  • Speed: ntrl-demo's label s = clip(d / margin, offset / margin, 1) with offset = margin / 10 (ntrl-demo's default margin is 1/30), stretched by its metric trainer to S = 1.025 s²(2 − s)² − 0.025, so the slowest speed (touching or inside a wall) is 0.012. Walls are slow, not solid.
  • True travel time: shortest paths over the car centre's (x, y) positions at θ = 0, on a 0.005 grid with extra lines at the bay centres and bay edges, 16 neighbours per point, each step costing its length times the average of 1 / S at its ends. Checked against the (x, y, θ) ground truth of the registered runs in nine scenes: the time to bay B's centre agrees to within 1.2%, the peak inside the wall to within 8%.
  • Along-line slope dT/ds: the difference in true travel time between neighbouring grid lines on x = 0.29, divided by their spacing (0.0025 to 0.005), signed so that positive means the time grows as the car moves toward bay B. The eikonal equation fixes the full gradient, |∇T| = 1 / S, so |dT/ds| ≤ 1 / S.
  • Learned field: each registered parking run's final checkpoint (84 fields: 3 training seeds for most scenes, ntrl-demo metric and, for the divider sweep, metric_arm), evaluated from the bay-A parked pose it was trained from: learned T = T(qA, q) along x = 0.29 at θ = 0 every 0.001, learned speed = 1 / |∇T| with the gradient taken in the trained coordinates q = (x, y, θ / 2π) as in training, learned dT/ds = −∂T/∂y. The learned heat map is the same speed on a 121 × 121 grid. Planning success is per training seed out of 300 (100 bay-to-bay queries × 3 planning seeds; widening runs report the fine MPPI). Data: jeffrey/parking/explorer/learned_data.py.
  • Straight-down time: the integral of 1 / S along the line x = 0.29 from the parked pose, i.e. crawling straight through the divider.
  • Slopes against car width: one shortest-path solve per width (0.01 to 0.225 in steps of 0.005) for the current divider, bays and margin. The steepest travel-time slope is the largest |ΔT/Δy| between neighbouring grid lines on the line; the speed slope is the largest |ΔS/Δy| on a 4,000-point sampling of it.
