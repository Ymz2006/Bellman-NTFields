2026-11-18 Sharp Features

Can sharp input features on top of the octave sin / cos embedding make walls easier to learn? Three ideas, each in several forms: even powers sinp, cosp (p = 2, 4, 8, 16), replacing some rows or added next to them; the triangle wave (8/π) arcsin(cos z); and that triangle switched on past an offset h by max(0, tanh(z − h)). Every field is scored against the exact octave trained with the same seed.

Final, 2026-11-18 16:20 EST. Sharp-feature sweep: 81 trainings (stopped at 12:00 for the midpoint follow-up). Midpoint follow-up: 20 trainings on 22×22 (seeds 87358 / 1 / 2).

Answer

  • No sharp feature beats the octave. The best ones tie it, exactly as plain features of the same count do: on 22×22, over three training seeds, the triangle pair added to every row +1.6 ± 2.0 points, a copy of the 128 rows +3.0 ± 2.0, 128 new octave rows +2.2 ± 2.0. The gate as written, max(0, tanh(z − h)) · (8/π) arcsin(cos z) with two random offsets per row: −4.5 ± 2.0 (−6.7 against the 128 extra plain rows); on 13×13 −0.4 ± 1.5. No gate variant (offsets at grid vertices or on every wall line, slopes, amplitudes, the gate alone, the gate on sin / cos) came out ahead.
  • Even powers lose at every p and in every form. Mixed p = 2…16 replacing half the rows: −14.0 ± 2.0 (22×22, 3 seeds), −22.2 ± 1.5 (13×13); added next to every row: −17.2 ± 2.0 and −18.8 ± 1.5, i.e. −19 / −21 against the same number of plain inputs; replacing every row: −40 (22×22, one seed per p).
  • The triangle as written loses through its shape, not its size. Replacing every row: −7.6 ± 2.0 (22×22), −11.2 ± 1.5 (13×13); sin / cos scaled to the triangle's size: +1.1 (one seed).
  • Why. The sharp features do what was hoped at the walls (with the powers added, runs through a wall fall from about 130 of 720 to 27, the speed fit and the training loss improve), but the learned travel time folds into a sawtooth: up to 21 % of the true path runs uphill toward the goal (the octave under 1 %) and the planner stops in the dips. The eikonal loss only constrains the size of the gradient, which a sawtooth satisfies. Even powers make the fold cheap because they are mirror-symmetric about every crest of their row: ±cos 2z alone (= sin², cos²) folds and costs 23 points, the full pair sin 2z, cos 2z does not (−10, under 1 % uphill), and the sign-keeping power |s|4 sgn(s) removes the local minima.
  • Putting the sharpness on the walls does not rescue it. On rows along the grid axes, whose crests and gate edges lie on every wall line, powers still lose 10–13 points and gates 17–19; the slow band the loss must resolve lies 0.15–0.36 cell from the wall line and moves with the robot's heading.
  • The midpoint loss does not rescue them. Forked at epoch 2,000 and trained with jeffrey/midpoint_dp's travel-time midpoint loss (22×22, three seeds), the exact octave goes from 46.3 to 54.9 % and 128 extra plain octave rows from 48.6 to 58.1 % (+3.2 ± 2.5 against the octave with the loss). The sharp features gain only 2–3 points from it and stay behind the octave with the loss: the gate as written −10.7 ± 2.5, the triangle as written −13.4 ± 2.5, the powers on half the rows −20.0 ± 2.5, the powers added −22.6 ± 2.5. Their travel time stays folded (the added powers keep 18 % of the true paths uphill) because the loss learns from the field's own traversals, and on a folded field almost all of them stall, so the long paths that would unfold the sawtooth never reach the loss.

Each of the 128 octave rows has a phase z = 2π B·(x, y, φ); the network reads sin z and cos z of every row (256 inputs). The features below act on that phase. Idea 1: powers of sin and cos

22×22, seed 87358, one training per spec unless noted (the exact octave of that seed averages 51.9 %). Every even power loses, at every p, in every form (three seeds: half the rows mixed −14.0 ± 2.0, added −17.2 ± 2.0). Half the rows replaced: 29–34 % for p = 2, 4, 8, 22 % for p = 16, 31 % for the mix; every row replaced: 5–12 %. Added next to every row (512 inputs): 26–29 % for p = 2 … 16, 24 % with all four powers (1,280 inputs), 28 % with the powers on the lower octaves only. The plain baselines of the same width: a copy of the rows 51.7 %, the rows at twice their frequency (sin 2z and cos 2z) 42.2 %, 128 new octave rows 43.3 %, 512 new octave rows (1,280 inputs) 54.0 %.

Two controls locate the cause. sin² z and cos² z are both ½ ∓ ½ cos 2z: a smooth cosine at twice the frequency, nothing sharp. Added next to the rows they still cost 23 points, while the full pair sin 2z, cos 2z (a superset of their information) costs 10, with the travel time folded in the first case (18 % of the true path uphill) and not in the second (under 1 %). And the sign-keeping power sgn(s) |s|4, as sharp as s4 but odd, replacing half the rows: 41.9 % against 33.5 %, with the local minima gone. So what hurts is that even powers are mirror-symmetric about every crest of their row; turned down to 0.3 or 0.1 of their size the added powers fold much less (41 %, 43 %), and gain nothing over plain features.

Best case: rows on the grid axes

The octave rows keep ntrl-demo's random directions, so a crest of sin16 or a kink of the triangle crosses the walls at an angle almost everywhere. Rows on the x and y axes at each octave level are the only ones whose crests, kinks and gate edges lie on the wall lines everywhere (a gate on them gets one edge on every grid line). Their plain sin / cos and gate-alone versions are the controls.

Interim (22×22, seed 87358). Aligning the sharp features with the walls does not rescue them. Plain sin / cos on the grid-axis rows: 52.8 %; the triangle on them: 52.9 % (a tie); sin⁴, cos⁴ on them: 41.4 % (7.5 % of the path uphill); sin¹⁶, cos¹⁶: 39.2 %; the gate with an edge on every wall line: 34.9 % alone, 32.6 % times the triangle (16 % of the path uphill). Before training the reason is already visible: the slow band the eikonal loss must resolve lies 0.15–0.36 cell from the wall line (half the wall plus the robot's half extent, which depends on its heading), so a crest or edge on the line itself sits in the wrong place (near-wall arc-length ratio 0.97–1.02 for every axis feature).

How the failures change

A failed run either reaches the goal only after crossing a wall (soft walls) or never reaches it (stopped against a wall or in a local minimum). Filled dot: change in the share of runs through a wall; open dot: change in runs that never arrive.

The even powers move the failures from walls to stalls: with the mixed powers added, the runs that reach the goal only through a wall fall from about 130 of 720 to 27, and the runs that never arrive rise from about 215 to 501.

reached the goal only through a wallnever reached the goal

Where the runs that never arrive stop (planning seed 0): moved less than 0.1 cell over the last 20 steps with no wall within 0.15 cell means the planner sits in a local minimum of the learned travel time in an open corridor. The exact octave stops there in 3–9 % of its runs.

Mechanism

The sharp features fit the training loss better and plan worse. With the powers added, the held-out speed correlation rises from 0.80–0.82 to 0.85, the near-wall slope from 0.62–0.71 to 0.76 and the final loss falls; yet the learned travel time has 11 local minima per long true path (exact octave 0.3–0.5). Along the true paths the eikonal residual |S |∇T| − 1| drops from 0.44–0.51 to 0.16, while 21 % of the path length runs uphill toward the goal (exact octave under 1.1 %). The eikonal loss only constrains the size of the gradient, so a travel time that zig-zags (a sawtooth) satisfies it as well as the true one. Two routes lead there: an input that is itself folded (even powers replacing rows: the network cannot build an odd function of those rows) and an input with kinks or narrow peaks (the triangle, the added powers), which make a sawtooth cheap to represent. The planner stops in its valleys.

With the midpoint loss

Each field is forked at epoch 2,000 of its run and trained to 5,000 with jeffrey/midpoint_dp's travel-time midpoint loss (its best setting: weight 1 ramped over 500 epochs, min leaves of one cell, traversals that keep out of walls, tree and sub-path nodes); the loss asks T(a, b) = T(a, c) + T(c, b) at travel-time midpoints c of the field's own MPPI traversals, so a sawtooth that satisfies the eikonal loss no longer satisfies it. 22×22, training seeds 87358 / 1 / 2 (the grid-axis powers and the dense top-octave gates: seed 87358 only).
