2026-11-17 Octave Detuning

Is the octave method good because its rows sit exactly on the octave frequencies f_grid / 2k, locked to the grid, or because it reaches higher frequencies than ntrl-demo? The test keeps everything of the octave matrix and moves the frequencies a little off the octave values, coherently (every row by the same factor) or row by row, and measures every outcome of the trained field, including how fast it converges.

Answer: the frequency level matters, the exact octave values matter little

All planned runs are in.

  • Sitting exactly on the octaves matters little for success. No single slight detuning that keeps the band where it is or moves it a little higher can be told apart from training noise. Each row moved by its own random ±5, ±10 or ±20 % (jitter): −2.0 ± 1.2, −0.2 ± 1.2 and −0.2 ± 1.2 points on 13×13, −2.7 ± 2.0, −1.6 ± 1.5 and −3.6 ± 2.0 on 22×22. Every row 5 % or 10 % higher: −1.0 ± 1.2 and −0.7 ± 1.2 on 13×13, −2.2 ± 1.5 and −1.7 ± 2.0 on 22×22. Pooled over these five and the fill below: −0.9 ± 0.7 points on 13×13 (94.8 % for the exact octave), no measurable change, and −2.2 ± 1.0 on 22×22 (48.1 %), a small loss at about two standard errors that comes with slower convergence (below). For scale, two trainings with the same frequencies to 1e-4 differ by 1.8 points (SD of one run) on 13×13 and 3.0 on 22×22.
  • No discrete frequency is needed at all. The log-uniform fill spreads every octave evenly over ±half an octave, so the 128 rows cover the band continuously: no two rows share a frequency and none is locked to the grid. It solves 92.7 % of the 13×13 runs (−1.1 ± 1.2 points) and 44.5 % of the 22×22 runs (−1.8 ± 2.0). What the octave method needs is rows across this band, not the octave values themselves.
  • Moving every row lower does matter, and the lock does not rescue it. 10 % lower: −5.0 ± 1.2 points on 13×13 (every seed lost) and −9.4 ± 2.0 on 22×22 (every seed lost); 10 % higher: −0.7 ± 1.2 and −1.7 ± 2.0. The two shifts break the lock by exactly the same amount (the top octave ends 2.0 rad out of phase at the 13×13 border, 3.5 rad at the 22×22 border), so the loss comes from the lower frequencies, not from the broken lock. 5 % lower sits in between: +0.6 ± 1.2 on 13×13 and −5.2 ± 1.5 on 22×22. Further down, success keeps falling, and ×0.5, back on the octave positions exactly one octave lower and locked to the grid, is the worst variant of all: −15.8 ± 1.2 points on 13×13 and −16.2 ± 2.0 on 22×22.
  • What the lower frequencies lose is the walls, and the top octave carries it. With every row 10 % lower, 9.3 % of the 13×13 runs reach the goal through a wall, against 2.3 % for the exact octave (43.5 % against 22.6 % on 22×22), and the training loss ends +15 % and +25 % higher. The wall barrier weakens as much near the maze centre as near the border, where a broken lock would show most (change in near-wall slope, outer minus inner third of the maze, for ×0.9 and ×0.95: +0.05 ± 0.03 on 13×13, −0.01 ± 0.03 on 22×22). The top octave is where the exact octave meets the finest scale of the maze, a half period of one cell. Lowering only the top octave by 10 % (the 13×13 part-of-band runs) reproduces the whole fit loss of the ×0.9 shift: training loss +15 % against +15 %, held-out speed correlation −0.021 against −0.018, and +3.6 against +6.7 points of wall crossings. Lowering every other octave by 10 % with the top exact changes nothing measurable (loss −2 %, wall crossings +0.4 points, success −0.2 ± 1.2). Raising the top octave above the cell scale (×1.1: half period 0.9 cell) costs nothing, so what matters is that the band reaches the cell scale, not that it sits on it.
  • Going higher than the octave gains no success, and far higher trades wall crossings for local minima. Every row ×1.19, ×1.41 and ×2 (locked again one octave up, a half period of half a cell): −1.5 ± 1.2, −2.8 ± 1.2 and −5.2 ± 1.2 points on 13×13, −1.1 ± 2.0, +1.9 ± 1.5 and −0.2 ± 1.5 on 22×22. At ×2 the walls hold: 0.6 % and 5.2 % of the runs reach the goal through a wall, against 2.3 % and 22.6 % for the exact octave, and the training loss ends −6 % and −16 %. But the learned travel time gains local minima along the true paths (+0.15 and +2.59 per path, where the exact octave has 0.04 and 0.57), and the planner stalls in them: the runs that never arrive change by +7.2 and +19.2 points, mostly stopped in open corridor, many of them on the true route. On 13×13, where the octave's runs rarely cross a wall, the stalls cost success; on 22×22, where 22.6 % of them do, the two roughly cancel.
  • What the exact values do buy: faster convergence on the larger maze and a slightly better fit. On 22×22, success at epoch 2,000 changes by −3.8 ± 1.0 points pooled over the six detunings that do not lower the band (the fill −6.2, ×0.9 −9.8); by epoch 5,000 the gap has narrowed to −2.2 ± 1.0, the small final loss above. On 13×13 the lag is small (−2.5 ± 1.3 at epoch 1,000, −2.0 ± 1.2 at 2,000). At epoch 5,000 the same six end with a higher training loss (+5 % on 13×13, +5 % on 22×22), a lower held-out speed correlation (−0.009 ± 0.002 and −0.020 ± 0.003) and a weaker near-wall slope (−0.04 ± 0.01 and −0.05 ± 0.01, target 1). These changes are consistent across variants but small: they cost no measurable success on 13×13 and about 2 points on 22×22. The scan agrees: every shift between the octave and ×2 (×1.05 to ×1.41) ends with a slightly higher training loss than the octave (+4 % at ×1.41 on 22×22), and on 22×22 its loss settles later the higher it sits (×1.41: 1,864 epochs later than the octave), while ×2, locked again, ends lower and settles only 486 epochs later.
  • The gain over ntrl-demo survives. Against the exact octave, ntrl-demo changes success by −35.0 ± 1.2 points on 13×13 and −23.0 ± 2.0 on 22×22; the detunings that do not lower the band keep nearly all of that gain.

What was changed

Each row of the matrix is one plane wave. Its dominant frequency, shown as the half period along the grid axis, sits on one of the octave positions (hairlines) in the exact method; the top octave has a half period of one cell. A shift moves every row by the same factor (orange lower, blue higher; ×0.5 and ×2 land on the octave positions again); a jitter (teal) moves each row by its own random factor, keeping each octave's geometric mean exact; the dashed rows move only part of the band. Rows, directions, heading column and network initialization are the same in every variant of a training seed. Hover a row for its lock error: the phase mismatch at the outermost grid line against an exactly locked row.

What fails along the scan

A run fails in one of two ways: it reaches the goal only after crossing a wall, or it never reaches the goal (the planner stalls, usually in a local minimum of the field). Each point is the change in one kind against the same seed's exact-frequency trainings, mean over training seeds, with ±2 standard errors from training noise as the whisker; the exact octave is the reference at zero.

The two kinds move in opposite directions. Lowering the band turns stalls into wall crossings: on 22×22, ×0.5 changes the wall crossings by +31.4 points and the runs that never arrive by −15.2. Raising it turns crossings into stalls: ×2 changes them by −19.0 and +19.2. On 13×13 the octave's runs rarely fail either way (2.3 % cross a wall); there ×0.5 adds +16.1 points of wall crossings and ×2 adds +7.2 points of runs that never arrive.

Where a run that never arrives stops tells the two causes apart. Rows run from the lowest factor at the top to the highest at the bottom; hover a dot for how many of the stops lie on the true route.

On 22×22 the exact octave's stalled runs mostly press against a wall (18.7 % of all runs, against 8.8 % in the open). The lower bands stop against a wall less often (8.2 % at ×0.5): their softer walls let the planner through, into the wall crossings above. ×2 turns this around: 29.9 % of its runs stop in the open corridor, and 16.6 % of all its runs stop there on the true route, against 2.8 % for the exact octave. Those runs were heading the right way and stalled in a ripple of the learned travel time. 13×13 shows the same on a smaller scale: 7.2 % of the ×2 runs stop in the open, against 1.2 %.

Where in the maze the change happens

A detuned row agrees with the locked row at the maze centre and drifts away from it toward the border (×1.1 ends 3.5 rad out of phase at the 22×22 border), so a lock effect would show as a wall barrier (near-wall slope, target 1) lost in the outer third of the maze and kept in the inner third. Each point is the change against the same seed's exact-frequency trainings, mean over the group's runs, with ±2 standard errors from training noise as the whisker; the zero line is the exact octave.

No detuning follows that pattern: each group loses about the same slope in every ring. Outer minus inner third, ×0.9 / ×0.95: +0.05 ± 0.03 on 13×13 and −0.01 ± 0.03 on 22×22; ×1.05 / ×1.1: −0.01 ± 0.03 and 0.00 ± 0.03; jitter: −0.01 ± 0.03 and 0.00 ± 0.03. The one difference beyond a standard error, the lower shifts on 13×13, goes the other way: they lose more slope at the centre (−0.08) than at the border (−0.04). What the detunings lose is spread over the whole maze, as a weaker fit would be, not gathered where the phase has drifted.

Method, controls and caveats

Mazes, data, network (656,900 parameters), losses, optimizer, 5,000 epochs, MPPI planner and exact scorer are those of jeffrey/maze_scale / jeffrey/grid_sync; only the Fourier matrix changes. 13×13: 200 queries × planning seeds 0/1/2; 22×22: 240 queries. Training seeds 87358 / 1 / 2 on 13×13 and 22×22, plus 3 / 4 on 22×22. Each (maze, seed) group runs on one GPU; the exact octave rerun on the 5070 is bit-identical to the grid-sync run.

Part of the band: top0.9 multiplies only the top octave's rows (the quarter of the rows on f_grid, 13×13) by 0.9 and leaves every other row exactly locked; rest0.9 multiplies every other row by 0.9 and keeps the top octave exact. Together they make the ×0.9 shift.

Failures are split by arrival (the planner appends the goal to every path, so the collision flag alone counts stalls as wall contacts). Near-wall slope: median S·|∇T| at held-out points with trained speed below 0.3 (target 1); minima per path: local minima of T along the true paths of queries of 8 cells or more.
