# Cross warp: a C-space warp in front of ntrl-demo's encoder, on the 22×22 cross maze

Request (2026-11-03): implement the idea of `README_warp.md` (a diffeomorphism φ of the configuration space placed
between the pose and the Fourier encoder, stretching space next to obstacles and compressing open free space, trained
with the field's losses, a free-space "suck-in" term and a Jacobian bound) on the cross maze of the maze-scale study
(`jeffrey/maze_scale`, artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/JuQxHyTA2siUza9a7ESHRv: 22×22 with a 6-cell + opened, where
ntrl-demo solves 67 % of runs). 17×17 or 20×20 were allowed; the user expected the idea to work and asked to keep going
until it beats the original metric NTFields baseline (ntrl-demo).

Everything below comes from registered runs (`report/tables.md`, generated by `report.py`; run ids at the end). Findings page:
`report/cross_warp.html`, published at https://claude.ai/artifact/4imv9v8epTaQV899Uz9dnp (private until shared).
Success = ntrl-demo's MPPI over planning seeds 0/1/2 on the maze-scale study's 240 certified plus-maze queries
(40 per true-distance bin), at the final epoch 5,000. Nothing here is committed.

## Setup

**Maze, data, planner, scorer** are `jeffrey/maze_scale`'s plus variant unchanged (prepare run
`20261102T042454Z-62864e61`): the 22×22 maze (seed 1022) with every interior wall inside a centred 6-cell + removed
(228 of 484 cells open, 290 walls left), a 0.5 × 0.2-cell rectangle robot in SE(2), q = (x, y, p) with
p = 0.25 θ / E, ntrl-demo's 500,000 training pairs (x0 within the 0.175-cell margin of a wall, x1 anywhere),
ntrl-demo's MPPI scaled per cell, success = reaching the goal without the robot touching a wall (exact clearance).
The oracle (the same MPPI on the true travel time) solves every query.

**Trainer.** `trainer.py` is ntrl-demo's `Model.train()` step for step (same global random-number calls in the same
order, 5 minibatches of 2,000 per epoch, its own `Function.Loss`, AdamW 5e-4 / weight decay 0.5, epoch rollback),
with the warp attached after the network's initialization. With the identity warp it **is** ntrl-demo: on the same
GPU it reproduces the registered ntrl-demo run bit for bit (seed 1 on the RTX 3090: maximum weight difference 0.0,
identical per-query outcomes, 479/720). On a different GPU the same seed diverges after a few epochs (floating-point
order), so "seed 87358" gives 468/720 on the RTX 5070 and 505/720 on the 3090: training noise of ±20 runs, which is
why every comparison below uses three training seeds.

**The long queries are one pocket.** All 40 queries of the 32-64-cell bin start in one dead-end pocket at the top of
the top-right quadrant and leave it along a corridor beside the outer wall before crossing the +. A field either
learns that escape or not, so the bin is all-or-nothing per field and moves totals by up to 120 runs. Tables show
the ≤ 32-cell bins separately, and a second query set (below) has 1,200 queries from 96 new goals.

**20×20 cross** (maze_scale `plusprepare --n 20`, run `20261103T190009Z-01bd3aac`): the same recipe on a 20×20 maze with a
6-cell +, 204 of 400 cells open, 240 certified queries; ntrl-demo there is the identity warp through `trainer.py`.

**Second query set** (`queries.py`, run `20261103T174058Z-405b526e`): 200 certified queries per bin from 96 new
goals and 200,000 new starts on the same maze (oracle 1,200/1,200, straight line 580/1,200), planned with planning
seed 0 by `evalq.py` for every trained field.

## The warp

T(x0, x1) = H(φ(x0), φ(x1)), H = ntrl-demo's network; φ is applied inside `NN.input_mapping`, so both endpoints,
the TD-shifted copies and every MPPI rollout state go through it, and the eikonal, normal and TD terms are taken in
x by autograd, exactly as without a warp (`warp.py`). φ is a diffeomorphism by construction:

    φ(x, y, p) = ( s ψ(x, y),  s_p exp(g(x', y')) p ),        (x', y') = ψ(x, y)

* **Coupling ψ** (README-faithful, `--xy`): two monotone conditional maps x' = F(x | y), y' = G(y | x'), each the
  normalized integral of a positive density on a node grid (one node per cell). It maps the box onto itself
  (resolution is only moved around) with each step's stretch bounded to [1/k, k] by construction; det J analytic.
* **Separable ψ** (`--sep`): x' = F(x), y' = G(y), 1-D monotone maps (no shear). Normalized to the box, or
  **unnormalized** (`--sep-free`): F' is the local magnification itself, so the warp can magnify the maze without
  shrinking anything else (the README's "limit the Jacobian to 2-3x", not a box constraint).
* **Global factors** s, s_p: with ψ = id, φ = s q is exactly the maze-scale study's B × s (checked to 4e-6), the
  uniform-stretch control.
* **Learned** (`--learn`): the grids are trained with ntrl-demo's loss through φ plus warp terms: free-space
  compression (README_warp's "suck in": det J ≤ 1/c at poses whose free radius is at least 1 cell, i.e. the open +),
  a near-wall stretch (det J ≥ c_b at ntrl-demo's x0, or the anisotropic form |J n| ≥ k along the clearance normal
  n, README_warp section 10), a curvature penalty on the log-densities, and the bound.
* **Designed** (`--design plusmag`): the separable unnormalized warp with magnification m_hi over the maze (the
  quadrant columns and rows and the + band's edge cells, whose outline walls must stay sharp) and m_lo = 1 over the
  + band's interior, log-linear over one cell; heading factor s_p = m_hi. `pm6` = ×6 / ×1.
