What the results say

Timing
LBS runs on the GPU with jeffrey/lbs_fast’s planner (CUDA graphs, 8 MPPI steps per call): 0.12–0.21 ms per MPPI step, 18–119 ms per solved query. ntrl-demo, RRT-Connect and LazyPRM run on two CPU threads. The same LBS fields with the earlier CPU planner (2 threads, 4.4 ms per step) took 0.6–1.75 s per query and solved the same share of queries (within 0.6 points in every cell, same path lengths); the fast planner draws different random numbers, nothing else changes.
2-D mazes
LBS solves 100 / 97.1 / 96.3 / 69.1 % at 10 / 14 / 18 / 22 cells against ntrl-demo’s 90.4 / 47.9 / 21.6 / 5.3 %, in 18–34 ms, with paths 1.03 × the shortest. With 30 s the sampling planners solve more on the large mazes (LazyPRM 99.1 %, RRT-Connect 84.9 % at 22 × 22) at 8.6 s and 13.6 s per solved query, and with paths 1.21–1.35 × the shortest. Nearly all of LBS’s 22 × 22 misses are stalls (447 of 464), not paths through walls, and they grow with distance: 79.3 % on the shorter half of the queries, 58.8 % on the longer half (LazyPRM 99.6 / 98.5 %).
Gap mazes
LBS 99.9 / 99.8 / 99.5 / 94.8 % at gap × 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 in 21–76 ms, against ntrl-demo’s 75.9 / 69.1 / 56.3 / 56.4 %, LazyPRM 100 / 100 / 99.9 / 97.3 % and RRT-Connect 100 / 99.1 / 96.8 / 85.8 %. The hard queries are the ones whose goal sits inside a maze block. At gap × 8, goal in the open band: LBS 100 %, RRT-Connect 99.7 %, ntrl-demo 98.8 %; goal inside a block: LBS 89.6 %, LazyPRM 94.7 %, RRT-Connect 72.0 %, ntrl-demo 14.3 %. Most RRT-Connect misses fail at both 10 s and 30 s: uniform samples land mostly in the wide band, so its tree rarely grows into the block’s corridors.
3-D houses
The sampling planners solve 82–100 % in 0.04–1.1 s (a point robot in open rooms is easy to sample); LBS 99.6–100 % in Collierville, Benevolence and Onaga, 70–91 % in five houses and 40–56 % in Allensville and Lakeville, in 34–119 ms, and almost never through a wall (2 of 1,000 misses). Its misses are queries that need a detour. Pooled over the 10 houses, by true path ÷ straight line: < 1.1, LBS 93.2 %, RRT-Connect 99.9 %; 1.1–1.5, 51.8 vs 95.8 %; ≥ 1.5, 24.3 vs 85.3 % (ntrl-demo 72.4 / 17.3 / 11.0 %). The field gets straight-line travel right and does not yet hold detours around walls in 3-D.
Time limit
Going from 10 s to 30 s per query moved RRT-Connect from 31 to 85 % on 22 × 22 and from 55 to 98 % on 18 × 18, and LazyPRM from 68 to 99 % on 22 × 22; RRT-Connect on gap × 4 from 86.5 to 96.8 % and on gap × 8 from 78.5 to 85.8 %. The houses and the 10 × 10 maze moved by 5 points or less. LBS has no time limit: MPPI runs until it arrives or its step budget ends; a failed LBS query costs its whole budget, which brings LBS’s mean over all queries (solved or not) to 59 ms on 22 × 22 and 0.33 s in Lakeville.

What was run

Scenes
Maze 2-D: 10, 14, 18, 22 cells a side, three mazes each (maze seeds 1000 i + n). Gap maze 2-D: the 10 × 10 maze cut into four blocks pushed apart by an open band of 6 k cells, k = 1, 2, 4, 8 (16, 22, 34, 58 cells a side), three base mazes each. The SE(2) rectangle robot. Gibson 3-D: a point robot flying in a 2 m slab of the main floor of ten houses: the five of the earlier 3-D test and the five houses with the smallest main floors (Collierville, Benevolence, Onaga, Coffeen, Forkland).
Queries
500 random (start, goal) pairs per scene: starts uniform over free space at least 0.1 cells from obstacles, goals drawn from the scene’s 48 random goal poses, every pair certified solvable by its ground-truth path. Gap mazes keep their goal mix (half of the goals inside the maze blocks, half in the open band). A table cell pools the three instances: 1,500 queries.
Planners
ntrl-demo and LBS: MPPI on the trained travel-time field (training seed 87358, epoch 5,000), planning seed 0, step budget 3 × the true path. ntrl-demo: ntrl-demo’s MPPI on the CPU (2 threads). LBS: the fast planner of jeffrey/lbs_fast (fast.Planner, variant goal, CUDA graphs, 8 MPPI steps per call, one random draw per call) on the RTX 5070, which ran nothing else during these runs; the same MPPI with the goal encoded once, so its paths are statistically, not numerically, the CPU planner’s. RRT-Connect: OMPL 2.0.1, default range. LazyPRM: a Python port of OMPL’s LazyPRM (k = 5 neighbours, lazy vertex and edge checks, a fresh roadmap per query). Both sampling planners start every query from scratch (no roadmap or tree kept between queries) and get 30 s per query, then OMPL’s path simplifier. (A first pass with 10 s per query is kept in the registry; the full tables give the share solved within 10 s.)
Success
The path reaches the goal and every configuration on it, at 0.005 cells per coordinate, is collision-free under the exact geometry. The sampling planners check motions at that same resolution, so their paths never fail the scorer.
Time and length
Wall time to the finished path (MPPI until it reaches the goal; sampling planners solve plus simplification). ntrl-demo, RRT-Connect and LazyPRM on two CPU threads while the GPUs trained other runs; LBS on the otherwise idle RTX 5070 (time from start and goal on the GPU to the path back on the CPU; one-off compile of about 20 s per scene not counted). Path length in cells: x-y length for the 2-D robot, x-y-z length in the houses (one cell is 0.25 m). Mean ± standard deviation over each planner’s solved queries.
