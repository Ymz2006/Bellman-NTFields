LBS on the cabinet arm

Lift, Bellman and segment (LBS) on the 6-joint UR5e in the cupboard scene, against the earlier metric NTFields (ntrl-demo) and P-NTFields runs on the same 600 frozen queries.

See the paths: a 3-D viewer shows the arm on every test query, following the certified path, the LBS plan and the ntrl-demo plan.

38 runs of this study are trained and scored by the cabinet evaluator, 13 on the two-arm scene by this study’s scorer.

The knobs

Range of h. 0.05: 60.1 %, 0.12: 63.2 %, 0.29: 61.5 %, 0.71: 58.7 %. The best is 0.12, the base setting; a narrower and a wider h each lose a few points. The values 0.29 and 0.71 are the base range times √6 and times 6 (h scaled up with the six degrees of freedom); 0.05 is the base range divided by √6. Without the lift (h removed, Bellman and segment kept) the run solves 28.9 %, below ntrl-demo on the same data (33.9 %).

Components. One removed at a time from the first LBS run (63.2 %): no lift 28.9 %, no Bellman 63.0 %, no segment 63.7 %. The segment loss adds nothing here: the run without it is within one point, which fits the training curves (flat from epoch 500, before the segment loss starts at 2,000). Without the Bellman update the result is within 0.2 points, so it can be dropped too. The lift alone (no Bellman update, no segment loss) solves 61.9 %, 1.3 points from all three together: on this scene the lift gives nearly all of the gain, and the two other terms together add a point or two at most (one training seed each).

Joint Fourier frequency. × 0.25: 64.8 %, × 0.5: 63.4 %, × 1: 63.2 %, × 2: 40.1 %, × 4: 21.7 %, by joint sensitivity: 61.6 %. A finer frequency breaks the field instead of sharpening it. At × 4, 543 of 600 plans (planning seed 0) do not reach the goal, against 208 at × 1. They stop at local minima of the field: for 440 of them no point within three planner steps has a lower time to the goal (63 of 208 at × 1). The median stop is 1.12 times the start-to-goal distance from the goal (0.10 at × 1), while the field’s time to the goal has fallen to 64 % of its starting value, so the field falls along the path without the arm getting closer. A coarser frequency (× 0.25, × 0.5) leaves the result where it is, so the result does not rest on detail at the scale of the base frequency either.

Training samples. A training set with 50 % of its pairs starting with the hand in the cupboard (3.8 % in the first set) gives LBS 63.8 % (first set 63.2 %) and ntrl-demo 32.2 % (first set 33.9 %). Drawing half of the segment loss’s endpoints inside the cupboard gives 63.2 %. Neither moves the result.

By suite

The earlier methods solve the suites in which most queries are already solved by a straight line (free → free, anywhere → anywhere) and almost none of the others. Compartment → another compartment always needs the arm to leave one compartment and enter another.

Why the first LBS run stops short

About a third of the plans of the first LBS run do not reach the goal (208 of 600 on planning seed 0). The cause is the field near goals that lie close to an obstacle, not the planner:

  • It depends on the goal’s clearance. Plans reach 92.9 % of the goals that are more than 50 mm from every obstacle and 21.0 % of the goals within 9 mm. The clearance of the start makes no difference. Half of the test goals have the hand inside the cupboard, at a median clearance of 14 mm.
  • The plans stop with a panel in the way. The median stop is 10 % of the start-to-goal joint distance short of the goal (0.29 rad), 12 mm from an obstacle, with the hand still 20 cm from its goal position. The straight move from there to the goal passes through the cupboard for 179 of 208.
  • The planner is not the limit. Three times the steps, eight times the samples, a longer horizon, another temperature or rejecting rollouts in contact solve between 381 and 409 of 600; a doubled step size solves fewer.
  • The approach side is wrong. Restarted on the suite’s certified path 8 planner steps before the goal, the same planner and field solve 172 of these 208 queries. So the field can guide the last stretch, but it leads the arm to the goal’s neighbourhood from a side that has no collision-free way in.
  • The field is close to plain distance near these goals. At the stop the field’s time to the goal is 0.032; on the certified path at the same distance from the goal it is 0.036. The dead end looks slightly closer than the way in.

The segment loss does not correct this. Its traversals ran between uniformly drawn free configurations at least 34 mm from every obstacle; 0.1 % of them have the hand in the cupboard, against 50 % of the test goals. Giving it such goals did not help: with half of the endpoints drawn with the hand inside the cupboard, down to the 5 mm contact limit, the run solves 1,138 of 1,800 (first run 1,138). Its traversals reach their goal 20 % of the time and stall otherwise, so the segment loss, which learns from traversals that get through, has little to learn from where it is needed.

Real-world cupboard (real_world_6dof)

The measured cupboard: 1.82 m tall, 0.92 m wide, 0.56 m deep, three zones 0.53 m tall behind an open front, standing on the floor. One UR5e with its gripper on a pedestal, base 0.70 m above the floor and 0.45 m in front of the cupboard. 600 new certified queries were generated with the cabinet recipe (six suites of 100; the certificate is a straight line or an RRT-Connect path that the release scorer accepts); a straight line solves 226 of them. Training and planner settings are those of the first LBS run of the cabinet. No earlier ntrl-demo or P-NTFields run exists for this scene, so the control is ntrl-demo trained here on the same data.

One change to the measured scene: the pedestal’s top is lowered by 4 cm. In the measured scene it touches the arm’s base, and the release scorer, which also checks the arm’s two fixed base spheres, then rejects every configuration.

LBS solves 63.8 % of the runs (1,149 of 1,800), ntrl-demo 35.0 % (630), a straight line 37.7 %. On the queries that need a detour: LBS 45.6 %, ntrl-demo 13.0 %. Zone → another zone: LBS 58 of 300, ntrl-demo 6 of 300.

The lift alone (no Bellman update, no segment loss) solves 64.6 % (1,162; detour runs 46.4 %), level with all three together, as on the cabinet.

Real-world cupboard, two arms (real_world_12dof)

The same measured cupboard with two UR5e arms side by side, 0.60 m apart, bases 0.60 m above the floor and 0.25 m in front of the cupboard. A configuration is 12 joints; the lift makes it 13 inputs. 600 certified queries in the six suites, the regions holding for both grasp points; a straight line solves 55 of them.

What differs from the one-arm scenes. (1) The release scorer and evaluator are for one arm, so these numbers come from this study’s own scorer: each arm by the release rule (5 mm from every obstacle, 2 mm self clearance), the two arms at least 5 mm apart, paths checked every 0.001 rad per joint. The same rule certified the suites; its distances agree with the separately written scene model to 0.3 µm. (2) The witness of a query is a straight line, an RRT-Connect path in all 12 joints, or one arm moving after the other; the suites keep only queries with such a witness (see the setup of this scene below). (3) Both pedestals are lowered by 4 cm, as in the one-arm scene.

Planner control. The same planner on the Euclidean field T = |x − g| (no network) arrives on every query and solves 118 of 1,800 runs (6.6 %): 113 of the 165 straight-line runs and 5 of the 1,635 detour runs. So the planner settings carry over to 12 joints, and a field has to beat 6.6 %, not 0. More samples or more steps change little (600 steps and 200 samples: 8.1 %, 2,400 steps and 50 samples: 6.6 %, 2,400 steps and 200 samples: 8.1 %). Run 20261121T211502Z-0611c599.

A first control that does not count: training pairs too short

The first training set drew a pair as in the one-arm scenes: a start near an obstacle, a step of uniform length up to 0.71 in a random direction, kept if the far end is not in collision. With two arms almost every long step ends in collision, so the kept pairs were short: median length 0.06, and 3.6 % as long as the median test query (0.46); in the one-arm real-world scene 22 % are. The long ones also ended outside the task box, where no query is. ntrl-demo trained on it solved 11 of 1,800 runs, fewer than the Euclidean field, and 57 % of the runs never reached the goal (run 20261121T210323Z-3ca3e6a4). The runs of this section use a second training set: the same rule, but the kept pairs fill 20 equal length bins of 0 to 0.71 equally, and in every bin half of the far ends have both grasp points in the task box (56 % in total, the share the one-arm scene has).

The control. ntrl-demo solves 1.7 % of the runs (30 of 1,800; 0.7 % of the detour runs); a straight line solves 9.2 %, the Euclidean field with the same planner 6.6 %. The control is below both: its field holds a third of the runs at the start and sends most of the others into contact.

The lift alone (no Bellman update, no segment loss) solves 10.8 % of the runs (194 of 1,800; 7.2 % of the detour runs): above every control, but far from the 62 % it reaches with one arm. Most of its runs stall before the goal, and the longer the query the more often: 42 % of the shortest fifth of the queries arrive (joint distance 0.17 to 0.40 of the joint range), 1 % of the longest fifth (0.55 to 0.80).

One knob changes this: Fourier frequency × 0.5. The lift alone then solves 37.4 % of the runs (673 of 1,800; 31.9 % of the detour runs), against 10.8 % at the setting of the one-arm scenes. Other knob runs: Fourier frequency × 0.25 37.0 %, h range 0.38 10.1 %, h range 0.06 7.8 %, h range 0.54 7.7 %. One training seed.

LBS (lift, Bellman, segment) solves 10.2 % of the runs (183 of 1,800; 7.7 % of the detour runs), level with the lift alone (194). In this run the segment loss had almost nothing to learn from: of the 4,096 training traversals of a refresh, between 3 and 23 reached their goal. The traversal pairs are random free configurations at most 0.85 apart, as in the one-arm scenes, and with 12 joints such pairs are almost all long: 90 % are longer than 0.65, and 95 % of the test queries are shorter than that. A field that arrives only on short queries never completes them, so the segment loss cannot start.

Traversal pairs of every length. Pairs taken from the training pairs instead (both ends free, lengths spread evenly over 0 to 0.71): lift + segment (no Bellman) 14.4 % of the runs (260 of 1,800; 10.7 % of the detour runs).

The other parts at half the frequency. ntrl-demo (control) 2.7 % of the runs (48 of 1,800; 1.0 % of the detour runs); lift only 37.4 % of the runs (673 of 1,800; 31.9 % of the detour runs); lift + segment (no Bellman), traversal lengths spread 37.7 % of the runs (679 of 1,800; 32.0 % of the detour runs); LBS (lift, Bellman, segment), traversal lengths spread 33.1 % of the runs (596 of 1,800; 28.4 % of the detour runs). The segment loss was active in its run (between 1,565 and 1,739 of the 4,096 training traversals of a refresh reached their goal) and it changes nothing measurable on top of the lift: 679 against 673 runs solved. Full LBS is below the lift alone, 596 against 673 runs, and more of its paths end in contact (62 against 16); the second seed, next, shows how much a seed moves these numbers. The lift is still what carries the result: without it, half the frequency gives 2.7 %. A second training seed (87359): lift only 639 (first seed 673), lift + segment (no Bellman) 628 (first seed 679), LBS (lift, Bellman, segment) 565 (first seed 596). The two seeds differ by 30 to 50 runs, so lift only and lift + segment are level. Full LBS is below the lift alone on both seeds, by 77 and 74 runs: with two arms the Bellman update costs planning at this frequency. At a quarter of the frequency full LBS solves 612 runs and the lift alone 666: lowering the frequency further does not close the gap. The control arrives almost always but 97 % of its paths are in contact: its field does not see the obstacles. In the runs with the lift at half the frequency almost no path ends in contact; the unsolved runs stop before the goal.

Close pairs: predicted against true travel time

A direct check of the values, not of planning. A pair is a valid configuration and the same configuration moved by a short step in a random direction, kept if the straight step stays collision-free; 5,000 pairs per step length. The true time is the travel time of that straight step at the training speed, the step cost the Bellman update uses; for steps this short it is the true travel time or a tight upper bound. A step of 1 cell is 0.063 of the joint range with one arm and 0.072 with two; the median test query is 0.30 and 0.46.

One arm. The lift-only field is close to the truth: median 1.02 at 1 cell, 81 % of the pairs within 20 %. ntrl-demo is 1.13; LBS overestimates, 1.43, with 18 % within 20 %. Two arms. No field is close: within 20 % are 18 % (ntrl-demo), 25 % (lift only) and 6 % (LBS, median 3.29). The medians of ntrl-demo and lift only hide two opposite errors: steps in open space are overestimated (1.88 and 1.52), steps next to obstacles underestimated (0.47 and 0.41), and with two arms 37 % of the steps are next to obstacles (one arm: 7 %).

The overestimate of LBS comes with the Bellman update. On the cabinet at 1 cell: lift only 0.98, lift + segment 0.99, lift + Bellman 1.23, all three 1.25. The segment loss with min leaves adds no bias of its own; it also does not remove the Bellman update’s.

The overestimate follows the Fourier frequency. LBS on the cabinet: frequency × 0.25 1.10, frequency × 0.5 1.08, frequency × 1 1.25, frequency × 2 2.02, frequency × 4 3.83. The two runs at a lower frequency are close to the truth and plan as well as the first run; the two at a higher frequency overestimate two- and four-fold and are the ones whose planning collapsed. The range of h changes little (1.24, 1.25, 1.30, 1.35).

With two arms half the frequency does not remove it. LBS at 1 cell goes from 3.29 to 1.65; in open space it still predicts 2.42 times the true time. The lift-only field at half the frequency has a median of 0.77, but that is again two opposite errors: 1.10 in open space and 0.32 next to obstacles; the segment loss leaves it there (0.80). At a quarter of the frequency LBS is 1.42 (1.95 in open space). Test run 78f55697.

Segment-loss versions on the one-arm scene

The question: can the segment loss remove the overestimate of LBS? The LBS runs above already use the main version of the midpoint-loss study: wall-avoiding drives, min leaves (a leaf is worth the smaller of the network’s time and the time the drive took over it), tree and sub-paths, weight 1, leaves of 1 cell. Each run below is full LBS on the one-arm real-world scene with one change.

A heavier segment loss lowers the overestimate, slowly: weight 1: 1.43, weight 3: 1.42, weight 10: 1.30, weight 30: 1.16, weight 100: 1.05 (lift only: 1.02). Planning does not change: 63.8 %, 63.5 %, 64.2 %, 64.3 %, 63.3 % of the runs solved. At weight 100 about 92 % of the gap to the lift-only field is closed, and the steps next to obstacles go down with the rest (median 0.36 of the truth, LBS 0.45, lift only 0.34): the segment loss lowers the whole field toward the lift-only field; it does not make the times next to obstacles right.

The leaf rule does not matter either: path leaves 1.44, net leaves 1.48. Letting the Bellman update also try the straight step to the goal gives 1.32. In the training drives the true time of the straight step across a leaf is 0.94 of the drive’s own time over it, so path leaves and min leaves are both close to the truth already; the leaves are not where the bias comes from.

Half the Fourier frequency lowers the overestimate and is the one change here that also improves planning. LBS at frequency × 0.5 predicts 1.11 of the true time (frequency × 1: 1.43), 69 % of the steps within 20 %, and it solves 1,242 of the 1,800 runs, against 1,149 at frequency × 1 (1,177 with another training seed). The lift alone at half the frequency: 0.99 and 1,181 runs (1,162 at frequency × 1). So at half the frequency LBS is ahead of the lift alone on this scene, by 61 runs. A second training seed of LBS at half the frequency: 1,290. The lift alone with the second seed at half the frequency: 1,146, so LBS is ahead of the lift alone on both seeds. The ntrl-demo control at half the frequency: 666 runs, about what it solves at its own frequency; the lower frequency helps only with the lift. A quarter of the frequency: 1,223 runs and 1.04 of the true time, so the values are closer still and planning is level with half the frequency. On the cabinet half the frequency did not change the LBS result.

Where the bias comes from. The Bellman update compares T(a, goal) with the true cost of a short step plus T at the step’s end. Evaluated on the lift-only field, whose open-space times are right, that target is on average 0.55 step radii above the field’s value (1.94 with two arms); adding the straight step to the goal barely moves it (0.51). The training pairs start next to obstacles, where the true step cost is high and every field here underestimates the time (see the close pairs by speed above). So the Bellman update is right to ask for more time there. My reading of what follows: the network does not put the extra time next to the obstacles; it raises the time everywhere until the average residual is zero (LBS: -0.02 radii), which is the overestimate in open space, while steps next to obstacles stay underestimated. Mean T over the training pairs: lift only 0.185, LBS 0.299; two arms 0.392 and 1.552. How far it raises it follows the Fourier frequency (chart above): on the cabinet, at half or a quarter of the frequency the LBS field is within 10 % of the truth; with two arms half the frequency halves the overestimate and does not remove it. Setup

  • Scene and queries: the table and cupboard of the cabinet task, the release UR5e with its gripper, the 600 frozen queries (six suites of 100). Final numbers come from the unchanged cabinet evaluator and release scorer (obstacle clearance at least 5 mm, self clearance at least 2 mm, joint limits).
  • Coordinates: each joint divided by its range, so every joint has range 1.
  • Lift onto 7 dimensions: the network’s input is the six joints and h, the travel-time height of the configuration computed from its clearance. At gain 1 the range of h is 0.12. The range of h and the frequency of its Fourier column are the same knob.
  • Bellman: the backup with the true step cost, the clearance evaluated exactly along every candidate step, on 512 of the 2,000 pairs of each minibatch.
  • Segment: the midpoint loss from epoch 2,000, 4,096 traversals per refresh between configurations at most 0.6 apart.
  • Speed model: the clearance is the obstacle distance, with self-contact as a thin-band wall. The release label (obstacle distance and half the self distance) is not used: with it the self term sets the speed of 86 % of the valid configurations.
  • Planner: ntrl-demo’s MPPI, 50 samples, horizon 5, 600 steps of 0.0034 (about 0.04 rad), joints clamped to their limits; the same for every run of this study.
