Does the midpoint loss fix what the lift alone cannot?

Added to the travel-time lift without the push, the recursive midpoint loss fixes the lift's short far field wherever it shows up, and its see-through detours only where the lifted field can already drive around obstacles. On the plus mazes and the houses, travel times along free lines of sight go from 0.54–0.88 of the truth to 0.82–0.97. Detours are learned in Gibson Allensville (43 cells): 84.6 % of the queries solved against 66.5 % for the lift alone and 83.9 % for ntrl-demo. On the plus mazes the field keeps going through walls and gains +4.3 / +0.8 / +1.4 points (plus maze 22 x 22 / plus maze 30 x 30 / plus maze 40 x 40). The loss learns only from drives that the current field makes. A field that sees through a wall drives into it and stalls, and its drives that do arrive are almost all straight lines.

Does the midpoint loss fix the short far field?
yesLine-of-sight travel times on the plus mazes and the houses, learned over true: 0.54–0.88 with the lift alone, 0.82–0.97 with the midpoint loss. In the configuration space the lifted field puts part of its slope into the heading coordinate and comes out short far away; drives along free lines of sight are what the loss sees most, and it corrects them. On the mazes the lift alone is already right along lines of sight (0.99 / 1.00).

Why not on the plus mazes?
no detours in the drivesThe loss trains on the drives the current field makes. On the 22-cell plus maze the lifted field's drives reach their goal 53 % of the time, and 1.0 % of those are detours (more than 30 % longer than the straight line); ntrl-demo's drives: 83 % and 6.8 %. In Gibson Allensville (43 cells) the lifted field's own drives already hold some detours (6.1 % of the arrivals) and the loss grows them: 76 % of its drives arrive at the end, 16.7 % of them detours. The same limit holds without the lift: on the 40-cell plus maze even ntrl-demo's drives arrive almost only along straight lines (0.3 % detours), and the midpoint loss on ntrl-demo's network stays at 60.7 % (ntrl-demo 59.2 %).
And when the drives do hold detours?
in the house, mostly yes; on the plus maze, partlyDriver control: the same lift + midpoint training with every drive made by the frozen ntrl-demo field of the scene, which arrives with detours (6.8 % of its arrivals on the 22-cell plus maze, 23.4 % in Gibson Allensville). In Gibson Allensville the lifted network then learns the long detours almost fully: a true detour of 17.1 cells is learned as 15.5 cells, against 5.7 when the lifted field drives itself and 17.7 for ntrl-demo's network with the midpoint loss; detour index 0.84 against 0.37. On the 22-cell plus maze it learns part of them: detour index 0.39 against 0.01 (ntrl-demo's network 1.11), 8.0-cell detours learned as 3.2 cells (ntrl-demo's network 8.8). Queries solved with ntrl-demo's drives: 87.5 % and 61.9 % (self-driven 84.6 % and 59.3 %). So in the house the missing piece is the detours in the data; on the plus maze it is also the representation: the lift gives the two faces of a 0.1-cell wall the same height, and the step in travel time across the wall has to come from the planar features alone (the lift-scale study's finding).
Is the lift worth adding to the midpoint loss?
noQueries solved, midpoint alone against lift + midpoint: maze 13 x 13 89.3 % against 88.7 %; maze 22 x 22 39.2 % against 31.7 %; plus maze 22 x 22 76.4 % against 59.3 %; plus maze 40 x 40 60.7 % against 57.6 %; Gibson Allensville 92.8 % against 84.6 %. Midpoint alone is ahead on maze 22 x 22, plus maze 22 x 22, plus maze 40 x 40, Gibson Allensville and level on maze 13 x 13, where the lifted field is not see-through. The lift removes ntrl-demo's wall crossings, but the midpoint loss needs a field whose drives go around walls, and ntrl-demo's smeared walls do that where the lift sees through.
What would make the combination work?
Both missing pieces are known from the lift-scale study: drives that hold detours from the start (a push, or a driver field that is not see-through) and planar resolution at the cell scale, so a thin wall can carry a step (octaves of the position features scaled with the scene took the houses to 98–100 % with lift + push). Neither was combined with the midpoint loss here.

What the midpoint loss changes in the field

The lifted field has two faults the lift-scale study measured: in the robot's configuration space it is short far away even where nothing is in the way, and it treats obstacles as bumps it can see through. The first chart is the first fault, the second the second.

Travel time along free lines of sight, learned over true (median)
Random pairs whose true path is within 5 % of the straight line; 1 is the truth.
Detour index on pairs whose true path is at least 30 % longer than the straight line (median)
(learned − straight) / (true − straight): 0 means the field goes straight through the obstacle, 1 the true detour.

The midpoint loss narrows the first fault on every open scene (0.54–0.88 → 0.82–0.97 of the truth): drives along free lines of sight are the drives a see-through field completes, and their travel times are what the loss pulls the field towards. The detour index moves only where detours are among those drives.

Why: the loss learns only from drives the current field can make

Every 100 epochs the loss drives 8,192 random pairs with the current field and learns travel times only along those drives. A pair whose straight line crosses a wall reaches its goal only if the field leads the drive around the wall. A field that sees through the wall leads it into the wall, where the drive stalls; the part before the stall is kept, and it says nothing about the way around. How many random pairs need a detour at all differs by scene: maze 10 x 10 64 %, maze 13 x 13 71 %, maze 22 x 22 65 %, plus maze 22 x 22 11 %, plus maze 30 x 30 10 %, plus maze 40 x 40 8 %, Gibson Allensville 22 %, Gibson Lakeville 12 %, Gibson Klickitat 13 %.

Training drives that reach their goal along a detour, % of the 8,192 drives of each refresh
A detour: the drive is more than 30 % longer than the straight line between its ends. One point per refresh, epochs 2,001–4,901; each panel has its own scale.

Per field, from its own drives (leafcheck.py, 4,096 drives of the final network): on the 22-cell plus maze the lifted field arrives 53 % of the time with 1.0 % detours, ntrl-demo 83 % with 6.8 %; on the 40-cell plus maze both fields' arrivals are straight (0.3 % and 0.3 % detours). In Gibson Allensville (43 cells) the lifted field arrives 31 % of the time with 6.1 % detours and, after the midpoint loss, 76 % with 16.7 %. On the 22-cell maze, where every corridor wall is seen through (detour index -0.63), only 6 % of the first refresh's drives arrive at all. On the 13-cell maze the lifted field is not see-through at all (detour index 1.18) and 72 % of its arrivals are detours.

A second, smaller effect is in the leaves. A leaf is worth min(network time, time the drive took); that minimum was built for ntrl-demo, whose field is long at the one-cell scale, so the drive's time usually wins and anchors the targets. The lifted field in the configuration space is short locally on the 22-cell plus maze: the network's time over a leaf is 0.98 of the drive's before the midpoint loss and 0.93 after it (ntrl-demo 1.09), and the minimum takes the network's own value 59 % and 72 % of the time. The targets inherit that shortfall, so the field ends at 0.91 of the truth along lines of sight, consistent but short. In Gibson Allensville (43 cells) it went the other way (0.88 → 1.03), once the drives there held enough detours.

How the runs end

The lift never arrives through a wall on any scene; its failures are stalls in front of walls with the goal behind them. ntrl-demo fails mostly by driving through walls. The midpoint loss on the lift turns stalls into arrivals where it learns detours.

How it was run

  • Robot and scenes: the SE(2) rectangle robot of every local maze study (0.5 × 0.2 cells, configuration (x, y, heading)), exact clearance, ntrl-demo's speed label (margin 0.175 cells, floor 0.012), in 2-D scenes: maze_scale's 10 × 10, 13 × 13 and 22 × 22 mazes, plus mazes of 22, 30 and 40 cells with a centred + of 6, 8 and 12 cells, and three Gibson houses as floor plans (Allensville 43 cells, Lakeville 83, Klickitat 96; 0.25 m per cell).
  • Lift: lift_scale's rebuild of the lift page. ntrl-demo's network gets one more input, the travel-time height h = G(d) / E of the configuration, G(d) = ∫dmargin √(1/S² − 1). No push, no octaves.
  • Midpoint loss: midpoint_dp's main setting, unchanged. From epoch 2,000, every 100 epochs ntrl-demo's MPPI drives 8,192 random pairs on the current field. Training drives keep 0.02 cells off the walls (rollouts that would enter get +100); each drive is cut where it collides or stalls. Each drive is split at travel-time midpoints down to one cell; a leaf is worth min(network time, time the drive took), and every node and as many random sub-paths get the sum of their leaves as target. The term is |log T − log target| on 8,192 nodes per update, weight ramped 0 → 1 over epochs 2,000–2,500, to epoch 5,000.
  • Same run up to the fork: lift + midpoint is the lift-alone run of the same seed until epoch 2,000, call for call (its loss equals the registered lift-alone run to every digit before the fork, and the 22-cell plus maze plans 129 / 240 at epoch 2,000 in both). The lifted network is evaluated in the batched drives as one matrix product on rows (x, y, heading, h); it matches ntrl-demo's own forward pass to 4 × 10−7.
  • Arms: ntrl-demo and lift alone are the registered runs of the earlier studies (new lift-alone runs on the three mazes); midpoint alone is midpoint_dp's main run on the mazes and this study's --lift none run elsewhere; lift + push is lift_scale's page schedule, shown for reference. Driver control: the lift + midpoint training with the drives made by the frozen ntrl-demo field of the scene instead of the network being trained; leaves, targets and everything else unchanged.
  • Scoring: the prepared queries of each scene (40 per true-distance bin, certified), ntrl-demo's MPPI with planning seeds 0, 1, 2, success = arriving without touching a wall (exact clearance); field measures on 24,000 random (configuration, goal) pairs against the prepared truth; training seed 87358 everywhere.

Caveats

  • One training seed per arm. Between seeds the same recipe moves by 3–6 points on these scenes (lift_scale, midpoint_dp), so differences below about 5 points are not resolved.
  • "2-D" was read as 2-D scenes with the robot every local study uses. With a point robot the lift is exact and the heading shortcut (the reason the lifted field is short far away) does not exist.
  • The midpoint setting was chosen on ntrl-demo fields (midpoint_dp, 13 × 13 maze) and is used unchanged; nothing was tuned for the lift.
  • In Allensville the lift + midpoint field was still improving at epoch 5,000 (80.4 % at 4,500, 84.6 % at 5,000 with planning seed 0); longer training was not run.
  • Field measures of the maze_scale ntrl-demo and midpoint_dp runs were computed afterwards on their final networks (posthoc.py), on the same truth pairs as every other arm.
